1. THE 60-SECOND OPENING PITCH (Word-for-Word Script)
================================================================================
(When Ma'am says: "Explain what you have done in Experiment 3.")

"Good morning / afternoon Ma'am. In Experiment 3, our aim was to perform 
comprehensive Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) and inferential Statistical 
Hypothesis Testing on customer support interactions using 50,000 tweets from 
the TWCS dataset.

We structured the experiment into four core stages:
1. Feature Extraction & Class Balance: Extracted 9 linguistic and sentiment 
   metrics, identifying a realistic 50:1 class imbalance between routine 
   inquiries and high-friction customer complaints.
2. Distribution Analysis & Outlier Detection: Evaluated central tendency and 
   spread, proving that customer tweet length follows a right-skewed Log-Normal 
   distribution rather than a standard Gaussian curve.
3. Feature Correlation: Mapped linear (Pearson) and non-linear (Spearman) 
   relationships across text length, sentiment scores, and punctuation intensity.
4. Rigorous Hypothesis Testing: Implemented 4 parametric and non-parametric tests:
   - Welch's two-sample t-test
   - One-Way ANOVA with Tukey HSD post-hoc
   - Chi-Square test of independence
   - Mann-Whitney U test

Our key finding is that frustrated and angry customers write significantly 
longer tweets (p < 10^-50) and that customer frustration surges during evening 
and night shifts (p = 0.00031). These insights provide concrete statistical 
foundations for downstream priority routing and balanced machine learning."

3. DEEP-DIVE ON THE 4 HYPOTHESIS TESTS
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TEST 1: TWO-SAMPLE WELCH'S INDEPENDENT t-TEST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Question: Do dissatisfied customers write longer tweets than happy customers?
- Hypotheses:
    H0: mu_complaint = mu_joy (Mean word counts are identical)
    H1: mu_complaint != mu_joy (Mean word counts differ significantly)
- Group Statistics:
    Complaints (Anger + Sadness): N1 = 8,531, Mean = 21.74 words, Std = 10.32
    Joy / Gratitude            : N2 = 9,703, Mean = 19.41 words, Std = 10.10
- Test Results:
    Welch's t-statistic : t = 15.3815 (degrees of freedom df = 17,896.2)
    Effect Size         : Cohen's d = 0.229 (Small-to-moderate practical effect)
    P-Value             : p = 4.78 x 10^-53 (p << 0.001)
- Decision: REJECT H0.
- Scientific Interpretation:
  "The probability that a difference of +2.33 words occurred by random chance is 
  virtually zero. Frustrated users provide elaborate problem descriptions, 
  error codes, and transaction numbers."

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TEST 2: ONE-WAY ANALYSIS OF VARIANCE (ANOVA) & TUKEY HSD POST-HOC
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Question: Does tweet length vary significantly across all five emotion classes?
- Hypotheses:
    H0: mu_1 = mu_2 = mu_3 = mu_4 = mu_5 (All emotion means are equal)
    H1: At least one emotion category has a statistically distinct mean word count
- Test Results:
    ANOVA F-statistic   : F = 254.4395 (Between df = 4, Within df = 49,995)
    P-Value             : p = 8.22 x 10^-217 (p << 0.001)
- Decision: REJECT H0.
- Tukey HSD Post-Hoc Findings:
  Pairwise comparisons confirm that Anger (+2.48 words, p < 10^-10) and Sadness 
  (+2.11 words, p < 10^-10) are statistically longer than both Joy and Neutral classes.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TEST 3: CHI-SQUARE (chi^2) TEST OF INDEPENDENCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Question: Is customer emotional state dependent on the time of day?
- Hypotheses:
    H0: Customer emotion is independent of Time of Day (Morning, Afternoon, Evening, Night)
    H1: Customer emotion is dependent on Time of Day
- Contingency Table: 5 Emotion Classes x 4 Time Bins (50,000 total observations)
- Test Results:
    Chi-Square Statistic: chi^2 = 36.1225 (df = 12)
    Cramer's V          : V = 0.0155
    P-Value             : p = 3.10 x 10^-4 (p = 0.00031 < 0.05)
- Decision: REJECT H0.
- Practical Takeaway:
  Customer emotion varies significantly by time period. Angry complaints surge 
  in evening and night shifts due to reduced customer support staffing and longer 
  response latencies.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TEST 4: MANN-WHITNEY U NON-PARAMETRIC RANK-SUM TEST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Question: Does complaint length stochastically dominate joy without assuming normality?
- Hypotheses:
    H0: The distributions of complaint and joy word counts are stochastically equal
    H1: Complaint word count stochastically exceeds joy word count
- Test Results:
    Mann-Whitney U      : U = 47,272,279.5
    P-Value             : p = 6.85 x 10^-62 (p << 0.001)
- Decision: REJECT H0.
- Methodological Value:
  Validates the Welch's t-test findings non-parametrically, ensuring that right-skewness 
  or extreme outliers did not distort our conclusions.

5. COMPREHENSIVE VIVA VOCE Q&A (Top 12 Questions & Master Answers)
================================================================================

Q1: Why did you use Welch's t-test instead of the standard Student's t-test?
A1: "Standard Student's t-test assumes both equal sample sizes and equal variances 
    (homoscedasticity). In our dataset, complaints (N = 8,531) and joy tweets 
    (N = 9,703) have unequal sample sizes and differing standard deviations. 
    Welch's t-test adjusts degrees of freedom using the Welch-Satterthwaite 
    equation and does not require equal variances."

Q2: What is the significance of the p-value being p = 4.78 x 10^-53?
A2: "The p-value is the probability of observing our test statistic (or something 
    more extreme) assuming the null hypothesis is true. A p-value of 10^-53 is 
    orders of magnitude below our alpha threshold of 0.05. This gives us overwhelming 
    statistical evidence to reject H0 and confirm that the length difference is 
    genuine and not random sampling noise."

Q3: Why did you run a Mann-Whitney U test if you already had the t-test?
A3: "Because our word count distribution is right-skewed (skewness = +1.02) and 
    strictly speaking violates the normality assumption of parametric t-tests. 
    The Mann-Whitney U test is a non-parametric rank-sum test that requires no 
    normality assumptions. Getting p = 6.85 x 10^-62 proved our conclusions are 
    fully robust."

Q4: Why is Log-Normal a better theoretical fit than Gaussian for text length?
A4: "Text length has two strict physical constraints: it cannot be negative (bounded at 0), 
    and most people write brief messages with a long tail of very descriptive 
    complaints. The Log-Normal distribution naturally models strictly positive, 
    right-skewed multiplicative processes, matching our empirical data with 
    KS-stat = 0.0893."

Q5: What was the purpose of Tukey's HSD post-hoc test after ANOVA?
A5: "A one-way ANOVA only tells us that AT LEAST ONE emotion group differs from the 
    others (omnibus test), but it cannot specify WHICH pairs differ. Tukey's HSD 
    (Honestly Significant Difference) performs pairwise comparisons while controlling 
    the family-wise error rate, proving that Anger and Sadness are specifically 
    longer than Joy and Neutral."

Q6: Why did you compute both Pearson and Spearman correlation matrices?
A6: "Pearson r evaluates purely linear relationships, which can be distorted by 
    outliers. Spearman rho calculates monotonic rank-order correlation, which captures 
    non-linear relationships and is invariant to skewness. Presenting both provides 
    a complete view of linear and monotonic dependencies."

Q7: What does the Chi-Square test tell us about time of day?
A7: "The Chi-Square test of independence (chi^2 = 36.12, df = 12, p = 0.00031) rejects 
    the null hypothesis that emotion is independent of time. It reveals that angry 
    and frustrated interactions surge during evening and night shifts, likely due to 
    lower support agent staffing levels and slower response times."

Q8: How did you calculate Tukey's outlier fence?
A8: "We calculated the Interquartile Range: IQR = Q3 - Q1 = 24.0 - 12.0 = 12.0 words.
    Upper Fence = Q3 + 1.5 * IQR = 24.0 + 1.5(12.0) = 42.0 words.
    Any tweet with more than 42 words is an outlier (1,799 tweets, 3.60%)."

Q9: Should we delete these 3.6% outlier tweets before training models?
A9: "No Ma'am. In NLP and customer support, long tweets are NOT measurement errors; 
    they represent complex, multi-issue customer grievances containing critical 
    troubleshooting information. Removing them would discard our most valuable 
    high-priority escalation training data."

Q10: What does the 50:1 class imbalance mean for future modeling?
A10: "It means that a naive classifier predicting 'Neutral' every time would achieve 
     62% accuracy while completely missing all frustrated customers. For downstream 
     experiments, we must use class-weighted cross-entropy loss, SMOTE/oversampling, 
     or evaluate with Macro F1-score rather than simple accuracy."

Q11: What is VADER and how does its compound score work?
A11: "VADER (Valence Aware Dictionary and sEntiment Reasoner) is a rule-based 
     sentiment tool specifically tuned for social media. It calculates positive, 
     negative, and neutral valence proportions and normalizes them into a compound 
     score ranging from -1.0 (extreme negative) to +1.0 (extreme positive)."

Q12: What are the primary open-source libraries used in this experiment?
A12: "We used Pandas and NumPy for feature engineering, Matplotlib, Seaborn, and 
     Plotly for visualizations, and SciPy (`scipy.stats`) and Statsmodels for 
     distribution fitting, ANOVA, t-tests, and Chi-Square computations."
