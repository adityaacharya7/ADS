  3. Exponential Fit:
     - Parameters: Scale β = 14.23
     - Kolmogorov-Smirnov Test: KS-Stat = 0.1640, p = 0.00e+00
  Diagnostic Interpretation:
     - The continuous text length distribution exhibits pronounced right-skew
       and positive kurtosis, rejecting pure normality. Q-Q diagnostics confirm
       the empirical distribution is best approximated by a right-skewed
       Log-Normal process before truncating at Twitter character limits.

B. Discrete Variable Distribution Fitting ('exclamation_count' - Poisson Fit):
  - Poisson Model: P(X = k) = (λ^k * e^-λ) / k!
  - Estimated Poisson Parameter: λ = 0.292 (Mean exclamation mark rate per tweet)
  - Chi-Square Goodness-of-Fit Test: χ² = 40,389.48, p < 1e-15
  - Interpretation: Exclamation mark counts represent zero-inflated discrete
    events where most tweets contain zero exclamations (k=0: 78.4%), while heated
    escalations display bursty over-dispersion.

C. Outlier Detection:
  1. Interquartile Range (IQR) Method:
     - Q1 = 12.0 words, Q3 = 24.0 words, IQR = 12.0 words
     - Lower Fence = max(0, Q1 - 1.5*IQR) = 0.0 words
     - Upper Fence = Q3 + 1.5*IQR = 42.0 words
     - Outlier Count: 1,799 samples (3.60% of dataset)
  2. Standard Deviation / Z-Score Method:
     - Threshold: |Z| > 3.0 (x > 48.27 words)
     - Outlier Count: 754 samples (1.51% of dataset)
  - Conclusion: Outliers represent detailed customer complaint narratives and multi-issue
    inquiries, which contain high informational value and must be preserved during NLP modeling.

6. HYPOTHESIS TESTING: T-TEST, ANOVA, CHI-SQUARE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Plot Asset: plots/exp3_hypothesis_tests.png

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HYPOTHESIS TEST 1: Welch's Two-Sample Independent t-Test
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Hypotheses & Test Statistic:
   - Null Hypothesis (H0): μ_complaint = μ_joy (Mean word count of dissatisfied
     complaints equals mean word count of gratitude tweets).
   - Alternative Hypothesis (H1): μ_complaint != μ_joy (Mean word counts differ).
   - Sample Sizes: N_complaint = 8,531 | N_joy = 9,703
   - Sample Means : x̄_complaint = 21.74 ± 10.32 words | x̄_joy = 19.41 ± 10.10 words
   - Test Statistic: Welch's t = 15.3815 (degrees of freedom df = 17,896.2)
   - Effect Size   : Cohen's d = 0.229 (Small-to-moderate practical effect)

2. P-Value and Interpretation:
   - p-value = 4.78 x 10^-53 (p << 0.001, far below significance threshold α = 0.05).
   - Interpretation: The probability of observing a difference of 2.33 words
     between complaints and gratitude tweets by random chance is virtually zero.

3. Conclusion based on Test Result:
   - Decision: REJECT NULL HYPOTHESIS (H0).
   - Conclusion: Frustrated customers systematically compose significantly longer
     inquiries to detail grievances, transaction context, and impact.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HYPOTHESIS TEST 2: One-Way Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) & Tukey HSD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Hypotheses & Test Statistic:
   - Null Hypothesis (H0): μ_1 = μ_2 = μ_3 = μ_4 = μ_5 (Mean word count is equal
     across all 5 emotion categories).
   - Alternative Hypothesis (H1): At least one emotion group has a different mean.
   - ANOVA Test Statistic: F = 254.4395 (Between-group df = 4, Within-group df = 49,995)

2. P-Value and Interpretation:
   - p-value = 8.22 x 10^-217 (p << 0.001, far below α = 0.05).
   - Interpretation: Variance in text length between emotion categories is
     statistically significant compared to within-group variance.

3. Conclusion based on Test Result:
   - Decision: REJECT NULL HYPOTHESIS (H0).
   - Tukey HSD Post-Hoc Analysis:
     * Anger vs Joy      : Mean Diff = +2.48 words, p < 1e-10 (Significant)
     * Sadness vs Joy    : Mean Diff = +2.11 words, p < 1e-10 (Significant)
     * Neutral vs Anger  : Mean Diff = -3.20 words, p < 1e-10 (Significant)
     * Fear vs Anger     : Mean Diff = -0.34 words, p = 0.812 (Not Significant)
   - Conclusion: Customer emotions drive distinct structural communication lengths.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HYPOTHESIS TEST 3: Chi-Square (χ²) Test of Independence & Cramér's V
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Hypotheses & Test Statistic:
   - Null Hypothesis (H0): Customer emotion category is independent of time of day
     (Morning, Afternoon, Evening, Night).
   - Alternative Hypothesis (H1): Customer emotion category is dependent on time of day.
   - Contingency Table Matrix (5 Emotion Categories x 4 Time Periods):
     +--------------------------+-----------+---------+---------+-------+
     | Emotion Category         | Afternoon | Evening | Morning | Night |
     +--------------------------+-----------+---------+---------+-------+
     | Anger / Frustration      | 1,123     | 1,443   | 797     | 1,586 |
     | Disappointment / Sadness | 869       | 1,033   | 657     | 1,023 |
     | Fear / Anxiety           | 154       | 184     | 121     | 164   |
     | Joy / Gratitude          | 2,417     | 2,852   | 1,695   | 2,739 |
     | Neutral / Inquiry        | 7,549     | 9,025   | 5,543   | 9,026 |
     +--------------------------+-----------+---------+---------+-------+
   - Test Statistic: Chi-Square χ² = 36.1225 (Degrees of freedom df = 12)
   - Effect Size   : Cramér's V = 0.0155

2. P-Value and Interpretation:
   - p-value = 3.10 x 10^-4 (p = 0.00031 < 0.05).
   - Interpretation: Statistically significant temporal dependency exists, although
     the practical effect size (V = 0.0155) is weak.

3. Conclusion based on Test Result:
   - Decision: REJECT NULL HYPOTHESIS (H0).
   - Conclusion: Anger and frustration rates peak during evening and night shifts
     (due to reduced staffing and cumulative flight/service delays).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HYPOTHESIS TEST 4: Mann-Whitney U Non-Parametric Rank-Sum Test
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Hypotheses & Test Statistic:
   - Null Hypothesis (H0): The distribution of word counts for complaints and joy
     are stochastically equal.
   - Alternative Hypothesis (H1): Complaints stochastically dominate joy in word count.
   - Test Statistic: Mann-Whitney U = 47,272,279.5

2. P-Value and Interpretation:
   - p-value = 6.85 x 10^-62 (p << 0.001).
   - Interpretation: Confirms robust non-parametric significance without assuming normality.

3. Conclusion based on Test Result:
   - Decision: REJECT NULL HYPOTHESIS (H0).
   - Conclusion: The median rank of complaint lengths is systematically higher than joy.

7. SUMMARY OF EDA INSIGHTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Class Imbalance: Neutral inquiries represent 62.3% of traffic; rare critical
   threats (Fear/Anxiety: 1.25%) necessitate class-weighted loss and multi-label models.
2. Structural Asymmetry: Dissatisfied customers write 12.0% more words (21.74 vs
   19.41 words, t=15.38, p<1e-50), providing actionable length-based triage cues.
3. Feature Collinearity: Word count and character count exhibit near-perfect
   collinearity (r=0.96), while VADER compound provides strong orthogonal polarity.
4. Theoretical Distributions: Continuous text length follows a right-skewed
   Log-Normal process; discrete punctuation follows a zero-inflated Poisson model (λ=0.29).
5. Temporal Dynamics: Night shifts experience elevated frustration rates (χ²=36.12,
   p=0.00031), informing agent staffing scheduling.
